# Image provenance

Every photograph in this directory is either public domain or under a licence
that requires no attribution, so nothing has to be credited on a slide. That
was a selection criterion, not luck: a Galton-board photo and a roulette-wheel
photo were both rejected for this set because they are CC BY / CC BY-SA and
would have put a credit line on the canvas.

Files are downscaled webp renders; the originals are far larger and live at
the source URLs.

## Rejected, and why

Worth recording so these do not get reintroduced.

**Container yard aerial, Port of Tacoma (US Army, public domain).** Downloaded,
placed on the two sample-count slides, then removed with those slides. The
photograph was fine; the slides were not. They were meant to show what a
sampling budget buys, and when three drawn attempts at that failed, a
quantity photograph went in and the argument came out, leaving slides whose
script could only describe their own picture. The beat was also already in
the deck twice, on the dartboard anchor and in the cost table.

**Hurricane Katrina (NASA, public domain).** Downloaded, placed on slide 25,
then removed. The licence was never the problem: Katrina killed roughly 1,400
people, and using a named disaster as decoration for a slide about sampling
is not a trade worth making in a room that will include people who lived
through it. The rule this establishes: no catastrophe as an
illustration, however free the image.

**Von Kármán vortex streets (NASA/NOAA, public domain).** Beautiful, and
wrong for the argument: a vortex street is periodic order emerging from
deterministic flow, so it illustrates almost the opposite of irreducible
randomness. Turbulence is the phenomenon this slide wants.

**Galton board (CC BY-SA 4.0) and roulette wheel (CC BY 2.0).** Both
conceptually apt, both would put a credit line on the canvas, and the
roulette photo is only 921 × 639.

## Paper crops for the oracle collage

hhl, qsvm, qrl, montecarlo, recsys, risk, confession, sgo, qnpg (and their
`-lg` variants) are 300 dpi crops of published arXiv papers, reproduced as
quotation for commentary: each scrap is annotated at the sentence where the
paper grants itself an oracle, and links to its arXiv source. The mapping
from crop to paper lives in `../collage.js`.

## Sources worth returning to

- NASA, NOAA, USGS: public domain outright, no attribution required.
- Wikimedia Commons: mixed licences. Check the file page, not the category.
- Unsplash: free commercial use, no attribution required.
- Fritzchens Fritz on Flickr: a large CC0 library of die photography, with
  Intel and Nvidia parts as well as AMD.

## Lesson 1 photographs added for the visual-less slides

Selection rule, unchanged: attribution-free licences only, so nothing needs
crediting on the canvas. Every one below is CC0, public domain, or carries a
public domain mark. Each entry records what it stands for and what it beat,
because these are metaphors and the reasoning is the part worth keeping.

### litmus-test.webp

Slide: diagnostic
Shows: Two strips of litmus paper, half blue and half red
Source: Wikimedia Commons, Litmus_paper.JPG
Licence: public domain
File: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Litmus_paper.JPG
Why this one: A litmus test is the standing idiom for one question that decides a classification, and the strips show both outcomes at once. Cropped to the strips; the original includes the bench and paper towel underneath.
